        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All five available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        How four years were read from the scans

        Every year of this San Francisco foundation's machine-readable filings reports its grantmaking as a single line pointing to “Statement D.” The IRS's rendered copies of four returns carry that statement — a register printed sideways inside the portrait pages — and 947 rows were read from the page images after turning them upright. Each year proves against the filing's own figures: the rows equal Part I line 25(d) and Statement D's own printed total, to the dollar, in all four years.

        Statement D carries a second money column for grants accrued for future payment, and those rows are excluded here as everywhere in this ledger — their printed totals ($25.1M, $18.4M, $27.4M, $44.2M) reconcile separately as a check. Twenty-two amounts the reading software could not see were read from the pages by hand, and two it read wrongly in a way no total would have caught: it returned $500 for a printed $2,500 and $500,000 for a printed $1,500,000, each time keeping only the tail of the number. Row alignment was proved geometrically rather than assumed — no amount on any page sits closer to a neighbouring row than to its own — and the row counts match the filer's own printed sequence numbers, including twenty-five unnumbered rows at the end of 2020 that open with a $23,000,000 grant to the Hellman Fellows Fund. The most recent year's scanned return is not posted yet and stays as the filed roll-up line. Purposes and addresses are transcribed as read and carry some scanning noise; no amount depends on them.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.
