        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All two available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        Which years are here, and how they were read

        The Triad Foundation’s machine-readable filings carry no grant rows at all — every year reports only totals, with the schedule left to an attachment. For 2021 and 2022 the IRS’s scanned copies include that attachment — a “Cash Grants Paid” report with every grantee, project title, payment date and amount — and those two years were read from the scans by optical character recognition, held to the same filed-total referee as every other ledger. Both prove completely: 565 rows summing to the filed $15,331,049 and 551 rows to the filed $16,123,223, each also matching the report’s own printed total to the dollar. Thirty-one amounts the OCR engine would not read — and one row it dropped whole — were read from the page images by hand; the totals prove them.

        The other three years are held out and named here rather than shown as unnamed lumps: the 2020 and 2023 scans do not include the report ($14,698,802 and $15,253,002 filed), and the 2024 scan is not yet posted ($15,343,408 filed). If the IRS posts copies that carry the report, those years will be recovered the same way.

        Recipient names on scan-read rows are as the report prints them; forty-five names the engine garbled were re-read from the page images by hand. Where a mailing city could not be read confidently, the location is left blank rather than guessed.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment. Nothing here is an audit, attestation, or opinion — it is a documented reconciliation of the foundation’s own public filings, with the receipts attached.
