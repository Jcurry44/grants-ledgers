        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. The single available year passes; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        Which years are here, and why

        Schusterman Family Philanthropies files every year’s grantmaking — roughly a third of a billion dollars annually — as a single “See Attached Statement” line, and the IRS’s published copies never include the statement. The foundation’s New York Charities Bureau filings do, as born-digital text, and they even list each recipient’s EIN. The 2021 year here was recovered from that copy: 688 rows summing to the statement’s own printed total of $292,518,510.97 to the cent, and to the filed Part I, line 25(d) of $292,518,511 in whole dollars — one grant refund (−$51,123.52, University of Virginia) is kept as the negative row the statement shows, and the three cent-carrying rows round so the year ties exactly.

        The other four years are held out and named rather than shown: 2020’s filing ($365,260,386) predates the AG’s posted window. And the 2022–2024 statements changed format — they list multi-year award commitments (summing $763.7M, $725.8M and $666.8M) rather than amounts paid in the year ($382.3M, $363.5M and $333.5M filed), so their rows reconcile to no filed total — not for this ledger, and not within the filings themselves. They are disclosed here instead; if a paid-basis statement is ever filed, those years will be added.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment. Nothing here is an audit, attestation, or opinion — it is a documented reconciliation of the foundation’s own public filings, with the receipts attached.
