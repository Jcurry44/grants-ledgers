        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All five available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        Three years read from the scans, refunds and all

        Three years' registers were recovered from the IRS's scanned copies, each proving section by section against the filer's own printed subtotals. This foundation's register is unusual in two ways worth stating. It reconciles to the cash column of the grants line rather than the books column — the two differ by millions in every year — so the cash column is the referee here. And it prints returned grants as negative rows: eighteen refunds across these years, carried exactly as filed rather than dropped, which is why a few rows below show money coming back. Each year also carries a printed line for grants passed through from private equity investments, named by the filer but attributed to no recipient; those are labeled as such. In 2021 the filer's own refund subtotal exceeds its own eight refund rows by one dollar — whole-dollar rounding in a small section — and that dollar is carried as a labeled row rather than pushed onto any printed row.

        2020 is withheld: that year's grants page reaches the public copy as a header band with nothing beneath it, and the filing carries no attachment pages at all, against twenty-three to twenty-five in each later year. Its $28.5 million would need a fuller copy. Approved-but-unpaid commitments are excluded throughout, each year reconciling exactly after the filer's own present-value discount.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.
