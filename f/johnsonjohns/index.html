        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All five available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        Two enormous registers, and what their own totals do not support

        This is the largest book in this collection. Three years were recovered from scanned filings of 573, 861 and 1,113 pages: an employee matching-gift register running to more than a thousand pages of small gifts — down to genuine gifts of $1.16 and 78 cents, kept as filed — plus a short register of large program grants (Save the Children's $5 million heads both years). Transcription was proven twice over each year: a second full reading at a different resolution, a glyph-level arbiter trained on the documents' own clean cells, and a pixel audit finding no uncovered ink on any of the 1,900-plus register pages.

        The finding is in the filer's own arithmetic. Each year the form carries two sub-totals — one for matching gifts, one for everything else — and the printed registers do not honour them. In 2022 the matching register settles against its sub-total to the cent, while the non-matching register itemizes $5,363,749 MORE than the sub-total it belongs to; no subset of its rows accounts for the excess, and no reclassification explains it, because the matching side already ties. Those 202 printed rows are therefore withheld from this ledger — publishing them would break the year against the filed total — and that year's non-matching giving appears as the single filed line instead. In 2021 and 2020 both registers miss their sub-totals in opposite directions that partly cancel, leaving those years' rows $1,690,243 and $3,855,408 short of the filed totals; each remainder is carried as one labeled row. The rule this ledger follows: every printed row is published when the rows fall short of the filed total, with the difference labeled; a section is withheld only when publishing it would carry the year past the total the foundation itself filed.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.
