        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All five available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        One year recovered from the scan; the rest say “detail upon request”

        Every year of this foundation's machine-readable filings reports its grants as one “DETAIL UPON REQUEST – SEE ATTACHED STATEMENT B” line. Only the 2021 public filing physically contains that statement — a nine-page scanned check register, sideways, at fax quality — and the 1,150 rows here were recovered from it: the page images were re-rendered upright, machine-read twice, and every amount in the register was then read by eye and cross-checked, because the scan defeated the OCR engine (dropped decimal points, dropped digits, fifty-six rows it could not see at all; the register's own printed total is an Excel “############” overflow burned into the page). The year proves exactly against the filed total: the register's rows sum to $20,461,302.04 and the filed whole-dollar line is $20,461,302 — one four-cent row is rounded down, and nothing else moves. A $62,800 refund row is kept negative as filed.

        The 2022 and 2023 public filings were read page by page and genuinely do not contain Statement B — “detail upon request” means exactly that — and 2020's and 2024's scanned copies are not posted by the IRS. Those four years are kept as the single roll-up rows the foundation filed, reconciling to the filed totals to the dollar: about $99.97 million of giving that is publicly untraceable, beside one year of 1,150 named checks.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.
