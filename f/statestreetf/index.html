        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All six available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        How four years were read from the scans

        Every one of this foundation’s machine-readable filings reports the whole of its grantmaking as a single line reading “SEE ATTACHED STATEMENT.” The IRS’s rendered copies of the 2018, 2021, 2022 and 2024 returns carry that statement — two registers printed sideways: the foundation’s direct grants, and the itemized matching-gift list its administrator distributes on its behalf — and 24,178 rows were read from the page images. In 2021, 2022 and 2024 the published rows are the direct grants plus the matching-gift list, each register reconciled to its own printed control to the cent and the year to Part I line 25(d) and Part XV line 3a to the dollar; the quarterly pass-through payments to the administrator that the list itemizes are kept in the evidence file rather than published twice. The 2018 copy is a degraded print: its direct register reconciles to every printed control and every recipient name was transcribed from the sheet, but its matching-gift list would not reconcile after five reading passes and is withheld, so that year shows the administrator’s pass-through rows exactly as the filer printed them. Fourteen amounts the reading software could not see were read from the page by hand; cents are apportioned to the whole-dollar filed line at no more than a dollar a row; a two-cent contradiction inside the 2024 filing and a credit row inside the 2022 list are carried as printed. No public copy exists for the 2020 and 2023 returns, and those years show their filed totals as single not-itemized rows. Rows without a printed state — many in the matching-gift lists — carry none, never a guess.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment. Nothing here is an audit, attestation, or opinion — it is a documented reconciliation of the foundation’s own public filings, with the receipts attached.
