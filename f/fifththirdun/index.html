        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All six available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        How four years were read from the scans

        This foundation’s tax year ends September 30, so year labels here always follow the filing, never the calendar. Every year reconciles to the filed total to the dollar.

        This foundation’s fiscal year ends September 30, so year labels here follow the filing: the year that began in 2019 is the return for the period ending September 2020. Every machine-readable filing reports the whole of its grantmaking as a single line reading “SEE SCHEDULE ATTACHED.” The IRS’s rendered copies of four returns — the years beginning 2019, 2020, 2022 and 2023 — carry that schedule, the trust department’s printed register, and 1,156 rows were read from the page images. Each recovered year proves against the register’s own printed total to the cent and against Part I line 25(d) and Part XV line 3a to the dollar, the filer’s cents apportioned to the whole-dollar line. Fourteen amounts the reading software could not see were read from the page by hand, and each year’s hand-read amounts equal that year’s shortfall to the cent, so none is unpriced. Twenty-nine state codes the software dropped were likewise read from the page; no state was ever inferred from a ZIP code. Matching-gift sections are published as the filer prints them, including the years in which the recipient named is the matching-gift administrator rather than the school. The register of grants approved for future payment is not part of this book. The public copy of the return for the year beginning 2021 is the IRS’s rendering of the e-filed data — 441 pages, none of them the schedule — and no copy has been posted for the year beginning 2024; those two years show their filed totals as single roll-up lines, itemized in no public copy.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.
