        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All three available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        How these years were read

        The Wyss Foundation's e-filed data reports each year's grantmaking as a single “See attached schedule” line; the schedules themselves exist only in the IRS's scanned copies of the returns. The rows here were read from those scans by optical character recognition and judged by the same referee as every other ledger: each year's rows must sum to the filed Part I line 25(d) to the dollar. All three years shown prove exactly, and each is corroborated twice — the schedule's own printed total matches the filed line in 2021 ($132,214,860), 2022 ($163,338,595), and 2023 ($149,332,735). Dozens of amounts the OCR engine would not read — among them the foundation's largest single grants, $33,527,382 to the Wyss Zurich Foundation and $18,591,426 to the Wyss Center for Bio & Neuro Engineering in Geneva — were read from the page images by hand; the printed totals prove them along with everything else.

        Two window years are not shown, each for a disclosed reason. For 2020 the schedule prints $125,722,675 and the recovered rows sum to $124,722,675 — one $1,000,000 row the OCR engine cannot see remains unlocated — and an inexact year is withheld, not published. The 2024 return (filed grants: $222,645,225) has no scanned copy posted by the IRS yet; its rows will be added when it appears.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment. Nothing here is an audit, attestation, or opinion — it is a documented reconciliation of the foundation’s own public filings, with the receipts attached.
