        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All three available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        Which years are here, and how they were read

        The Moore Charitable Foundation files every year’s grantmaking as a single “See statement” line, with the grants in an attachment. For 2021–2023 the IRS’s scanned copies include that attachment, and those years were recovered from the scans — transcribed page by page against the images, with optical character recognition as a cross-check, because on this scan the OCR engine silently dropped round amounts and misread digits. The referee decides either way: 297, 271 and 273 rows sum to the filed $30,592,564, $21,470,674 and $21,995,785 to the dollar — and for the two years whose statements print their own totals, to those as well.

        Two rows are grant refunds and appear as the negative amounts the statement shows. Recipient names, cities and the filer’s own typos are kept exactly as printed — including names the statement itself truncates — and rows the statement prints without any address carry a blank location rather than a guess.

        The 2020 and 2024 years are held out and named here rather than shown as unnamed lumps: the 2020 scan does not include the statement ($16,113,120 filed), and the 2024 scan is not yet posted ($23,407,209 filed). If the IRS posts copies that carry them, those years will be recovered the same way.

        Bridging to other public figures

        Summary sites often report a foundation’s total charitable disbursements — the grants this ledger counts (Part I, line 25(d)) plus the foundation’s own charitable operating costs (line 24(d)). These e-filings do not populate line 26(d), so the totals below are computed sums of the two filed lines and are labeled as such:

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment. Nothing here is an audit, attestation, or opinion — it is a documented reconciliation of the foundation’s own public filings, with the receipts attached.
