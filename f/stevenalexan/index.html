        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All five available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        How 2020 was read from the scan

        In this foundation’s returns the grants schedule and Part I line 25(d) are not the same number, so the referee here is stated plainly: the schedule’s own filed total, Part XIV line 3a. Every year’s rows sum to it exactly. The two figures as filed, year by year:

        This ledger does not explain the difference, because the filings do not; it shows both filed numbers.

        The 2020 return reports its whole year — $91,464,675 — as a single “See attachment” line in the machine-readable filing. The IRS’s rendered copy carries the attachment: a landscape grant register printed sideways inside the portrait filing, twelve sheets the filer paginates itself (“Page 1” through “Page 12”). Turned upright and re-read, it yields the 132 recipients shown here, and the year proves three ways — the rows equal the register’s own printed total, the schedule’s line 3a figure, and Part I line 25 in both the books column and the cash column, to the dollar. Ten amounts the reading software either could not see or shredded were read from the page images by hand and checked against three independent machine passes, among them the year’s largest row: $38,000,000 to Cohen Veterans Network, the family’s own veterans clinic network, which the filing flags as a related organization rather than an arm’s-length grantee.

        A second attachment in the same return lists $102,863,704 of grants approved for future payment. Those are commitments, not money paid, and are excluded here as they are everywhere in this ledger. Filer spellings are kept as printed and not corrected: Virginia Polytechnic Institute’s address reads “Blackburg, VA,” the University of California at San Diego is given a Chicago address, and NYU Langone’s address prints a city with no state, so no state is shown.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.
