        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All two available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        This ledger was read from a scan

        Every other ledger in this collection is built from the machine-readable XML the foundation e-filed. This one could not be: M&T’s returns report each entire year of grantmaking as a single line — “See attached statement” — and the individual grants live only in attachments the IRS publishes as scanned images with no text layer.

        So the rows here were read off those scans by optical character recognition, page by page, and judged by the same referee as every other ledger: the recovered rows must sum to Part I, line 25(d) of the filing. 2023 recovers completely — 3,105 grants matching both the statement’s own printed total and the filed line to the dollar. 2022 recovers 99.98%: 3,302 grants totaling $41,188,851 against a filed $41,196,491. The $7,640 the scan would not render legibly — roughly thirty rows — appears in the ledger as one explicitly labeled “unread” line rather than being hidden or guessed at, so the year still sums to the filed total exactly and the gap itself stays visible.

        A handful of recipient names in both years were too faint to read and are shown as unread rather than guessed; their amounts are included, because the amounts are what the totals prove.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step, because the source carries no purpose text at all: when neither step matches, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the name matches exactly one organization nationwide, when every same-named listing carries the same classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment. Nothing here is an audit, attestation, or opinion — it is a documented reconciliation of the foundation’s own public filings, with the receipts attached.
