        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All six available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        How three years were read from the scans

        This foundation’s machine-readable filings report each year’s grantmaking as a single line pointing to an attached schedule. The IRS’s rendered copies of the 2020, 2021 and 2022 returns carry that schedule — a printed register of about twenty-two sheets a year — and 791 rows were read from the page images. Each recovered year proves three ways: the rows equal Part I line 25(d), Part XV line 3a and the register’s own printed total, to the dollar. In 2020 the filer’s whole-dollar printing leaves the rows one dollar short of the filed line, and that dollar is carried as its own labelled row rather than hidden inside a recipient’s amount. Nine amounts the reading software could not see were read from the page by hand and checked against the printed total, and every one of the 2020 register’s 275 amounts was checked against the page. Where the filer printed no state, none is shown; the filer’s own spellings are transcribed, not corrected. The rendered copy of the 2018 return is the IRS’s rendering of the e-filed data alone, with no attachment, and no copy has been posted for 2023 or 2024: those three years show their filed totals as single not-itemized rows, because their lists exist in no public copy.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment.
