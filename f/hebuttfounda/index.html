        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All four available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        The H. E. Butt Foundation is an operating foundation — the Butt family's camps and retreat programs in the Texas Hill Country (Laity Lodge and its kin) are run in-house, and the grants here are its modest outbound giving. Two years itemize in the machine-readable filings; 2022's “SEE ATTACHMENT” line resolves to a one-page giving list inside the IRS's rendered copy, and its 78 rows were read from the page image, summing to the attachment's own printed $2,481,713.75, which the filed line rounds to $2,481,714 (the page labels itself Attachment 22 while the filing's stub says Attachment 20 — the filer's label mismatch, noted). 2020's roll-up line stays as filed — its scanned copy is not posted. In 2024 the schedule's rows and total run $10,705 above the cash line 25(d) — two of the filing's own figures, bridged below. The IRS's machine-readable indexes carry no 2021 filing for this foundation at all; that gap is stated rather than papered over.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.
