        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All six available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        How three years were read from the scans

        This foundation’s tax year ends August 31, so year labels here always follow the filing, never the calendar. Every year reconciles to the filed total to the dollar.

        This foundation’s fiscal year ends August 31, so year labels here follow the filing: the year that began in 2020 is the return for the period ending August 2021. Every machine-readable filing reports the whole of its grantmaking as a single line reading “SEE ATTACHED SCHEDULE.” The IRS’s rendered copies of three returns — the years beginning 2020, 2021 and 2022 — carry that schedule, a printed register of eleven to thirteen sheets, and 1,540 rows were read from the page images. Each recovered year proves three ways: the rows equal Part I line 25(d), Part XIV line 3a and the register’s own printed “Total Contributions Paid,” to the dollar, and every printed fund-section subtotal ties as well. Seventeen amounts the reading software could not see — every seven-figure cell among them — were read from the page by hand, and each is priced by the section subtotal it closes to zero. One quirk of the paper is disclosed rather than hidden: on two sheets the name column prints a line above its own amounts, so names and amounts were paired by order and checked against the other two years by city. Recipients abroad — 351 rows, most in Israel and the Czech Republic — carry their city as printed and no state, and the filer’s own spellings are kept. The separate register of contributions approved for future payment is not part of this book. No rendered copy has been posted for the years beginning 2019, 2023 and 2024; their filed totals stand as single roll-up lines, itemized in no public copy.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record. On this page there is a third step: when the filed purpose names no field and the recipient’s name matches no published rule, the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File (its NTEE code) is used — accepted only when the filing’s own recipient state pins the name to exactly one organization, when the name is nationally unique, when every same-named listing carries one classification, or when all listings are chapters of a single group exemption. Names with conflicting listings stay unassigned, as do IRS groups with no honest home in these categories.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment.
