The rows that exist nowhere else.

When a foundation attaches its grant list to the return as paper, the IRS e-file data carries one line — “see attached” — and every database built on that data carries nothing. These are the attachments, read from the IRS’s posted scans and reconciled to each filed total to the dollar. Nothing is estimated; a year that does not tie is not shown.

How a scanned year is read

The page images are read twice by local OCR at different resolutions and an amount is kept only when the two readings agree. Cells they disagree on are re-read at four further resolutions; cells no resolution can read are read by eye from the page image and recorded as such. The year is published only when the rows sum to Part I line 25(d) exactly, with cents apportioned at no more than one dollar on any row. Every published year names its method on its own page.

Who this is for

Prospect research and advancement teams screening funders that look quiet in commercial databases; foundation staff and boards who want their own record read back as a book; the digitizers and data services whose pipelines carry these foundations as a single unitemized line; and reporters checking a claim against the filing itself. The method is published, the receipts are attached, and the sample pack is free to read.
