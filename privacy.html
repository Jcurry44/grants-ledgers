What this site collects
about you: nothing

The Grants Ledger is a static website — plain files served over the web, built from public IRS filings. It has no accounts, no login, no forms, no cookies and no analytics. This page describes the actual data flows, verified against the site’s own code, so that a reviewer can check the claim rather than take it.

The short version

I receive nothing about you. I could not tell you how many people have visited this site, because nothing here measures it.

—
Accounts, logins, sign-ups
None exist. There is nothing on this site to register for.
—
Forms of any kind
There is not a single <form> element on the site. Nothing collects a name, an email address, or any other input.
—
Cookies
This site sets no cookies. It never reads or writes document.cookie.
—
Analytics or tracking
No analytics script of any kind is loaded — no Google Analytics, no tag manager, no Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Hotjar, Segment, Mixpanel or PostHog. No tracking pixel, no beacon, no session recording.
—
Advertising or data sales
There is no advertising on this site and no data about visitors to sell, share or transfer, because none is gathered.
—
Fingerprinting or cross-site tracking
None. The referrer policy on every page is strict-origin-when-cross-origin, so the pages you view are not passed along in full to other sites.
●
Saved lists and notes
Stored in your own browser only, and never sent anywhere. Details below.
●
Ordinary web-server request logs
Kept by the hosting provider, as with any website. Details below.

What is stored in your browser

The collection page lets you save foundations and recipients to a list and attach your own notes to them. That is the only feature on the site that remembers anything, and it remembers it on your device.

Stored valueWhereWhat it holdsLeaves your browser?
gl_listbrowser localStorageThe identifiers of the foundations and recipients you saved to your list.No. Never transmitted.
gl_notesbrowser localStorageThe notes you typed against items on your list.No. Never transmitted.

These two values live in your browser’s local storage for this site only. They are not synchronised to any server, not shared between your devices, and not visible to anyone but you. No copy reaches the maintainer of this site at any point.

You can delete them at any time by clearing site data for this domain in your browser settings. They will also be absent in a private window, or in a different browser, or on a different device — which is the ordinary consequence of storing them locally rather than in an account.

Exports — the CSV, spreadsheet and report files the site can produce — are generated inside your browser from data already downloaded to it, and are written directly to your device. Nothing about an export is transmitted or recorded.

Who else necessarily sees your request

Two third parties are involved in serving these pages. Neither is a choice made to gather information, and both are named here so a reviewer does not have to discover them by inspecting the page.

GitHub Pages — hosting

The site is served by GitHub Pages, a static file host operated by GitHub, Inc. Every request for a page, image or data file on this site therefore reaches GitHub’s servers, which receive the request in the ordinary way that any web host does — including your IP address, the file requested, the time, and your browser’s user-agent string. Those logs belong to GitHub and are governed by GitHub’s own privacy practices; the maintainer of this site has no access to them and receives no report derived from them.

Google Fonts — the three typefaces

The pages load their typefaces (Fraunces, Newsreader and IBM Plex Mono) from Google’s font service at fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com. Those requests go to Google and, like any web request, carry your IP address and user-agent string. This is the only third-party asset on the site; there is no other external script, stylesheet, image, frame or endpoint anywhere on it.

If your organisation’s policy is to avoid third-party font requests, this is the single dependency to raise — the fonts can be self-hosted instead. Say so and it will be changed.

Everything else the site loads comes from the site itself. All of its data requests are relative paths to files on this same domain — the collection database, the search index, the map geometry. There is no application programming interface call to any outside service, no content delivery network beyond the host, and no request whose destination is not one of the two above.

Links that leave the site

For completeness: each foundation’s ledger page carries ordinary outbound links to the source record — the foundation’s filings on irs.gov and its entry on ProPublica’s Nonprofit Explorer — so that any figure can be checked against the original. Those are links, not embedded content. Nothing is requested from either site unless you click, and no frame, script, image or pixel is loaded from them at any time.

If you do click, you are on that site under its own policies. Because every page here sets strict-origin-when-cross-origin, what is passed along is this site’s address, not the particular page you were reading.

If you email me

Email is the only way to contact this project, and it is the only circumstance in which the maintainer holds anything about you at all: your email address and whatever you chose to write.

That correspondence is used to answer you and, where you reported an error, to record what was checked and changed. It is not added to a mailing list — no mailing list exists. It is not used for marketing, not shared with anyone, and not sold. If you would like your correspondence deleted, ask, and it will be.

About the people named in the data

This site publishes information from Form 990-PF, the annual return that private foundations file with the Internal Revenue Service and which the IRS makes available for public inspection. The foundations, recipient organisations, amounts, purposes and addresses shown are reproduced from those public documents as filed.

Nothing is added to them. No filing is enriched with information from another source about any person, no profile of any individual is constructed, and no attempt is made to identify anyone. Where a filing itself names a person or lists an address, that is the public document’s own content, shown as it was filed so that the record can be checked against its source.

If you believe something reproduced here from a public filing is being shown in error, write, and it will be looked at against the original filing.

What is deliberately not claimed here

A trust page is worth less than nothing if it promises a regime that has not been implemented. These are the honest boundaries.

—
No compliance certification
This project holds no SOC 2 report, no ISO 27001 certificate, and no third-party security audit. None has been performed. The security posture is that of a static site with no server, no database, no user accounts and no stored personal data.
—
No formal GDPR or CCPA programme
No data-protection officer, representative, or formal request-handling process has been appointed or built. What is true instead is that the site collects no personal data, so there is no store of it to access, port or erase. Correspondence you send by email is the sole exception, and it is deleted on request.
—
No data-processing agreement is offered today
Because no visitor data is processed, none has been drafted. If your organisation requires one before using this site, raise it and it will be worked through rather than waved away.
—
No cookie banner, because there is nothing to consent to
The absence of a banner here is not an oversight. The site sets no cookies and runs no tracking, so there is no consent to collect.
