Everything that has changed,

Compiled from the project’s own version-control history. Foundations added, years recovered from paper, years withheld, and — kept in deliberately — the defects that were found and the fixes that closed them. A changelog that only listed additions would be advertising.

The Grants Ledger began on 23 August 2026 with a single foundation, and what follows is its complete history — nothing before this list exists. The pace below is real and is not the ongoing rate: the collection was assembled in an intensive build, and it now moves to a monthly refresh cycle described on the about page.

Entries are grouped by day and summarised from the project’s commit history. Figures quoted are the figures recorded at the time of the change.

5 Sep 2026Current · research workspace

A clearer workspace for finding and reviewing funders

More room for the research, from the first result to a finished brief.

  • Changed
    Results take the main screen. Comparable funder rows, readable controls, sortable results, compact mobile filters and an optional geography view. The green, cream and copper palette remains.
  • Added
    Review matching source grants in place. View evidence opens the same filtered rows behind each result. Returning preserves your search position. Saving carries the criteria and source examples into your shortlist; changed criteria can explicitly update saved evidence.
  • Improved
    A visible shortlist workflow. Persistent saved counts, inline project creation and renaming, clearer brief actions, and keyboard navigation with focus returning to the result you opened.
5 Sep 2026

Precise research, saved evidence and measured coverage

A clearer path from a comparable nonprofit to a funder shortlist and a source-backed brief.

  • Fixed
    Filters and reports count the same grant rows. Amount, year and location intersect; medians retain repeated amounts. Unitemized disclosures remain in filed totals and are excluded from named-recipient analytics.
  • Added
    Adjustable funder suggestions. Sector signals are separate from endowment, capital and operating uses. Suggestions show the matching scope and link to filed examples; no empty selection silently widens.
  • Added
    Research briefs with saved evidence. Project objectives, matching criteria, source examples and notes travel with the saved project. Briefs are printable and exportable. Mobile shortlist layouts are improved.
  • Changed
    Coverage measured at release. Named and unitemized rows, identity coverage, missing locations and filing/check dates come from the dataset. Identity and geography limitations are stated explicitly. Release checks compare source rows, pages and indexes before publication.

31 Aug 2026

The collection reaches 1,933 foundations

The top-2,000 sprint closes, the largest recovered book in the collection lands, and a fleet-wide geography defect is found and corrected.

  • Added
    Top-2,000 sprint closed. 1,933 foundations live, reconciling to $305.1B, with a recovery queue of 190 books still outstanding.
  • Added
    Johnson & Johnson — the collection’s largest ledger. 187,513 rows across three recovered years. Its 2021 year alone is 62,600 rows, proven by two independent reading engines with zero disagreements across 60,250 comparisons. Its 2022 register overshoots its own sub-control by $5.36M, so that year is withheld with the evidence published.
  • Added
    Recovery waves A through D. Bezos (419 rows; 2021 withheld, attachment absent), Musk 2022 (31 rows exact), Roberts (three years; 2021 truncated mid-alphabet, a $25M residual finding), MetLife (866 rows across three years), Caterpillar (13,736 rows), Amon Carter (581 rows), Pennington (204 rows), Pritzker, Grainger and others.
  • Fixed
    612 rows across 170 foundations carried a recipient location that was not the recipient’s. A roll-up stub row inherits the filer’s own address, not a grantee’s. Those locations were blanked and the pages rebuilt.
  • Fixed
    The map was hardcoded to New York. Left over from the collection’s New-York-only period: state shading, and every state zoom, used New York’s geometry. Rebuilt from real home states across 51 jurisdictions, with per-state zoom from actual path geometry.
  • Fixed
    Shading no longer relies on colour vision. The map uses a luminance ramp rather than a hue ramp, with a labelled legend.
  • Added
    The collection page becomes a working instrument. A one-viewport command deck; state dossiers on a real Albers-USA projection with per-city placement for 94.4% of foundations; purpose drill-downs; and a query index of 79,231 purpose-by-place-by-funder cells that answers “who funds work like mine”.
  • Added
    Peer lens. For any organisation, which funders support organisations like it and are not yet funding it — matched on grant scale, so a $25,000 grantee is not shown a billion-dollar funder.
  • Added
    Findings page, exports and a report builder. The recovery campaign’s catches organised by class; genuine spreadsheet, CSV and printable report exports generated inside the browser.
  • Fixed
    The canonical address had been serving a months-old page. Deploys wrote one filename but not the other, so the front door sat on a superseded build while every deploy reported success. Two sub-pages were serving stale copies with unrendered template placeholders. A pre-deploy gate now checks published-versus-source drift, the page-count invariant, visible placeholders and text defects, and refuses the deploy on any of them.

26 Aug 2026

One shared database, and the monthly refresh

The collection stops being a set of separate pages and becomes a queryable record — and starts keeping itself current.

  • Added
    A single shared database across the collection, designed to carry 15,000 foundations. Cross-foundation search, recipients and totals become possible for the first time.
  • Added
    The monthly freshness job. Pulls the current IRS index, fetches filings not already held, re-reconciles, rebuilds and redeploys. Anything the standing rules cannot settle is reported for review rather than published, and an amended return never silently replaces a published year.
  • Added
    The map, and a rebuilt exceptions page computed from the data rather than written by hand.
  • Fixed
    Hub links could 404. A foundation built between deploy waves was listed in the regenerated database but its page was never copied to the site. A deploy invariant now requires the published page count to equal the registry count.
25 Aug 2026

Western New York finished; the fleet becomes rebuildable

The first billion reconciled, a third classification tier, and the identity system that stops two organisations from being merged into one.

  • Added
    Western New York complete — 35 ledgers, $1,084,991,827, every buildable foundation above $10M in assets.
  • Added
    A third classification tier. Grants that keyword and name rules cannot classify are matched to the recipient’s own IRS Business Master File classification, accepted only when unambiguous. On one foundation this cut unclassified grants from 66% to 23.9%.
  • Added
    Recipient identity becomes a first-class system. Three tiers with one shared normalisation, identity by EIN from the Business Master File, and a short reviewed merge list with stated reasons. Names resolving to different EINs are never joined — a guard that keeps genuinely separate organisations one token apart from collapsing into each other.
  • Added
    New York’s first tranche closed — 23 giants, 118,622 grants, $29,656,404,986. The Ford Foundation goes live.
  • Fixed
    36 separate repositories become one. Every ledger moves under a single address. The old repositories remain as hash-preserving redirects so links already sent to foundations keep working.
  • Added
    The collection becomes reproducible. Every page rebuilds from a frozen template, a per-foundation configuration and the parsed filings. Proven by rebuilding all 27 existing pages and confirming they came back identical on every metric.
24 Aug 2026

One foundation becomes a collection

The hub launches, scanned paper is proven recoverable, and the exceptions page — the product’s spine — is published.

  • Added
    Foundations 2 through 14, and the hub. The collection passes $927,302,618 reconciled, with 456 organisations funded by two or more foundations visible for the first time.
  • Added
    Scanned paper proven recoverable. A foundation that files each entire year as a single line — “see attached statement” — had 3,105 grants read out of the scanned attachment and tied to line 25(d) to the dollar. Figures too faint to read are labelled unread rather than guessed.
  • Added
    The first schedule-referee ledger. A foundation whose two filed grant totals disagree in all five years is published against the grants schedule’s own total, with both figures and every difference printed on its face.
  • Added
    The exceptions page. Every withheld year, every excluded year, every judgement call and every standing limit, in one place.
  • Fixed
    Unclassified grants cut from 50.9% to 6.7%, with every classification rule printed on the page that runs it.
  • Fixed
    Accessibility. Zero automated violations, plus a screen-reader walkthrough run by hand, and a dollar-composition graphic redesigned so it decodes without colour vision.

How this page is produced

Entries are summarised from the project’s version-control history for the Grants Ledger source tree — 276 recorded changes between 23 August 2026 and 31 August 2026. Routine work is grouped; nothing that changed a published figure, a disclosure, or a stated limit is omitted. Where a change corrected a defect that had reached the live site, the entry says so.

From here the collection moves to the monthly refresh described on the about page, and this page will record what each refresh changed.
