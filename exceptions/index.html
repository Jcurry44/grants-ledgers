Standing limits

True of every ledger in the collection, on every page, always.

No recipient EINsForm 990-PF does not ask for the recipient’s EIN, so organizations are matched by name, through a published canonical form, with the filer’s own EIN anchoring identity where the IRS Business Master File pins it. Every as-filed spelling stays visible on the recipient’s own row.
Capitalization changes mid-periodSome foundations switch from filing names in capitals to mixed case partway through — the Western New York Foundation (36 of its 37 merges) and Ralph C. Wilson, Jr. (202 of 205) are the clearest. Those merges are the same name, differently typed.
Purposes are grouped by published rules, in three tiersEvery ledger’s Methodology prints the rules it actually runs: keywords in the filed purpose, then the recipient’s filed name, then — for names neither step matches — the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File, accepted only when the match is unambiguous. Where nothing matches, the grant stays in “other” instead of being assigned a guess.
Addresses are mailing addressesThe location on a grant row is where the recipient receives mail, not where the money is spent.
Amounts are as filedNo inflation adjustment, no restatement, no netting of refunds.
Nothing here is an auditThese are documented reconciliations of public filings, with the receipts attached. They are not an audit, an attestation, or an opinion.
