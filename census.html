Where the grants aren't.

Every private foundation files a grant total with the IRS. This census asked 7,962 of them — the tier below the ledgers, assets between $12.8 million and the collection's floor — one question, filing by filing: does that total have grant rows behind it in the public XML? Most do. These don't.

What the filings said

43,751 foundation-years were read. The classes below are exhaustive; every year is in exactly one of them, and nothing was estimated.

36,469
tie to the dollar

The grant rows in the XML sum to Part I line 25(d) exactly. This is the ledger standard, and it is the normal case: the public data is complete for these years.

900
tie to the schedule total (3a) instead

Rows sum to the schedule's own filed total but not to 25(d) — a definitional gap inside the filing, usually accrual against cash. Complete lists; not counted here as missing.

3,318
pointer years

One or a few rows that read “See attached”, “Schedule attached”, “See statement” or “Various” — carrying the whole total, often to the dollar, and naming no grantee. The list exists only as an attachment the XML never carried.

53
zero-row and partial years

41 years file a total and no rows at all; 12 list some grants and fall short of the total by more than rounding.

2,990
filed no giving

Line 25(d) is zero: operating foundations, trusts between distributions, first years. Nothing to reconcile.

$26B
set aside · 3 patient-assistance programs

Glaxosmithkline Patient Access Programs Foundation; Merck Patient Assistance Program Inc; Johnson & Johnson Patient Assistance Foundation Inc. Medicines given to patients, filed as one line. No recipient list exists anywhere for these, by design and by privacy. Counted, never “recoverable”.

254 foundations are missing $10 million or more

Largest first. “Scans” means the IRS has published a page-image PDF for the stubbed years — the only place the list can still be read. A scan existing does not guarantee the attachment is inside it; that is settled book by book, and the ledger publishes only what reconciles. Where a book has since been opened, the last column links to its ledger and says how many years came back.

How it was asked

For each foundation the census read every 990-PF e-filing the IRS has posted (the latest six periods), took Part I, line 25, column (d) — contributions, gifts and grants paid — and compared it to the grant rows in the filing's own schedule. It also read the schedule's own total (Part XIV line 3a) so a definitional difference is never mistaken for a missing list.

The rule that decided the count: a single row reading “See attached” for the whole total ties to the dollar and is still not a list. Pointer rows are judged before the tie test. Read the other way round, this census finds 55 gap years instead of 3,375.

The bandFoundations with $12.8 million to $86.2 million in assets on file — the tier immediately below the collection's own ledgers, which cover everything above.
Nothing estimatedEvery dollar figure is a filed line 25(d) or the amount printed on a pointer row. Where rows fall short, only the shortfall is counted.
Scans probed, not readFor the 400 largest gaps the census checked whether the IRS has published a scan for each stubbed year. Reading them is the recovery lane's work, one book at a time, reconciled to the same line.
What is not hereForm 990 filers (community foundations, corporate giving programs, public charities), foundations below $12.8 million in assets, and 38 filings the IRS lists but has not published in a batch.

Why it matters

The public e-file datasets that every grants database is built on carry only what the XML carries. When a foundation attaches its list instead of typing it, the money is real, the filing is complete, and the recipients are invisible to anyone who did not open the scan. Candid, in its November 2025 comments to the IRS, reports more than 2,000 such cases among funders giving $500,000 or more — and that in many of them the PDF has no list either. This page is the private-foundation half of that number, computed rather than sampled, and the collection's recovery findings are what happens when the scans are opened.
