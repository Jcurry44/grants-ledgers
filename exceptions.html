Everything that did not go cleanly

1944 foundations, 1,564,217 grants, $306,755,923,091 reconciled — and this page is the rest of the truth: every year withheld, every year excluded, every judgement call, and every known limit. A reconciliation product that only published its successes would not be one.

Ledgers that reconcile to a different filed line

1631 of the 1944 ledgers tie every published year to Part I, line 25(d) exactly. The other 313 reconcile instead to the grants schedule’s own filed total (Part XIV, line 3a), because in their returns the two filed numbers are not the same. That is a definitional difference inside the filing, not an error in it — and it is disclosed, never averaged: each of those ledgers names its referee on its face and prints both figures. The clearest case is The Ralph C. Wilson, Jr. Foundation, where the two lines differ in every year:

Standing limits

True of every ledger in the collection, on every page, always.

No recipient EINsForm 990-PF does not ask for the recipient’s EIN, so organisations are matched by name, through a published canonical form, with the filer’s own EIN anchoring identity where the IRS Business Master File pins it. Every as-filed spelling stays visible on the recipient’s own row.
Capitalisation changes mid-periodSome foundations switch from filing names in capitals to mixed case partway through — the Western New York Foundation (36 of its 37 merges) and Ralph C. Wilson, Jr. (202 of 205) are the clearest. Those merges are the same name, differently typed.
Purposes are grouped by published rules, in three tiersEvery ledger’s Methodology prints the rules it actually runs: keywords in the filed purpose, then the recipient’s filed name, then — for names neither step matches — the recipient’s own classification in the IRS Business Master File, accepted only when the match is unambiguous. Where nothing matches, the grant stays in “other” instead of being assigned a guess.
Addresses are mailing addressesThe location on a grant row is where the recipient receives mail, not where the money is spent.
Amounts are as filedNo inflation adjustment, no restatement, no netting of refunds.
Nothing here is an auditThese are documented reconciliations of public filings, with the receipts attached. They are not an audit, an attestation, or an opinion.
