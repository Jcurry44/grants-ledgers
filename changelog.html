Update history

Everything that has changed, with the dates it changed

Compiled from the project’s own version-control history. Foundations added, years recovered from paper, years withheld, and — kept in deliberately — the defects that were found and the fixes that closed them. A changelog that only listed additions would be advertising.

The Grants Ledger began on 23 August 2026 with a single foundation, and what follows is its complete history — nothing before this list exists. The pace below is real and is not the ongoing rate: the collection was assembled in an intensive build, and it now moves to a monthly refresh cycle described on the about page.

Entries are grouped by day and summarized from the project’s commit history. Figures quoted are the figures recorded at the time of the change.

24 Sep 2026Current

One product, one header, and the double rule as its signature

One product, one header. The hub, all 1,935 ledgers and every editorial page now share one header — the mark, Find funders, Explore, Shortlist, More, Method and Build a brief — and one set of type, spacing, focus rings and pressed states. Any ledger can be saved to the shortlist from its own page.

The double rule is the signature. The two lines an accountant rules under a reconciled total now also mark the current tab, draw the loading indicator and head the printed report. It carries meaning by shape, never by colour.

Years drawn as years. A foundation’s annual totals are columns, one per filed year, with the amount printed on each; a withheld or unitemized year is drawn in outline and named. Hover a year in the rail, the chart or the reconciliation table and the other two answer. Chart labels hold their size on a phone.

Find funders, faster. The first screen leads with the claim; “Any purpose” is the default, and grant-size and tax-year filters now work across every purpose. On a phone, search takes the screen and keeps its suggestions above the keyboard, and the search you ran is still there when you come back from a ledger. Panels that wait on data show it instead of a dash.

Links that introduce themselves. The 300 largest ledgers carry their own share card — the foundation’s name and its reconciled total — so a forwarded link previews the record itself.

Editorial pages read on a phone. Wide tables on Census and Recovered become cards under 640px, scrollable tables say so, and footer links are full-size tap targets.

23 Sep 2026

Ledger pages that show where each year came from, and two new publishing rules

Each ledger says what it was read from. Where a year’s grant list exists only as a scanned attachment, the page now says so under the headline — for Hellman, “Four of five filed years were read from the paper filing — absent from every e-file dataset” — with a link to how they were recovered. A withheld year now counts in the headline badge (“4 of 5 filed years reconciled · 1 withheld”) instead of appearing only in the year rail. The EIN links to the foundation’s filings on ProPublica’s Nonprofit Explorer. A book with fewer than ten named-recipient rows no longer prints a median, a typical-grant range or a concentration share, and says why.

A way to ask. Every ledger’s overview ends with two lines: one for research teams who want a foundation’s paper-only years recovered (the first year is free), and one for the foundation itself. Both open an email with the page’s address already in it.

Names of individual people are withheld on the ledger pages too. Scholarship, fellowship, loan and hardship grants to named people now read “Individual recipient (name withheld)” on the foundation’s own page, its print report and its CSV export, as they already did in search and the bulk file — 33,682 rows across 318 foundations. Amounts, years, purposes and states stay as filed, so every year still reconciles to the dollar; the city is blanked.

Foundations of financial-services companies are no longer published. Seventeen corporate foundations of banks, brokerages, asset managers and payment companies were removed under a conflict-of-interest rule: the publisher works in financial services. The collection is now 1,935 foundations, 1,446,980 filed rows and $304,011,800,391.

The hub says what it is. The first screen states the claim — every year reconciles to the filed total, to the dollar, exceptions disclosed — with a link to how, and three example searches. On a phone the result count no longer collides with the sort control and the claim is shown there too. Enter opens the top foundation; the search suggestions draw above the page. The hub and the editorial pages carry a social card and a canonical address.

Corrections. The privacy page said no analytics script was loaded; the pages do load GoatCounter, a cookie-free page counter, and the page now says so and describes what it records. The recovered-rows page no longer says those rows exist “nowhere else”; it says they are absent from every e-file dataset. Two lines in the hub’s findings rotation used a word the terms page retires; they no longer do.

13 Sep 2026

A second round on the live site: thirty-five more things fixed

The heaviest books read right. A year with thousands of named grants and one residual line no longer reads as “Incomplete detail”; only a year with no named rows does. The Methodology tie-card names the referee the page actually uses. The one-line fit sentence can no longer exceed 100%, and when a book’s latest year has no named rows it says so instead of vanishing.

The bulk file is honest to the row. The full-collection CSV now carries the foundation’s slug, its record type (so a lump-sum disclosure line is never mistaken for a recipient) and the IRS object ID or scan reference for every row, with a data dictionary beside the download. Lump-sum lines no longer appear as recipients in the search index. Every CSV writer defuses a leading formula character.

Locations that were fragments are blank, never guessed. Ninety-five rows on Hellman, Burroughs Wellcome and MetLife were re-read from the filings themselves; 1,246 more across the fleet whose city cell held a street, suite, date or floor fragment are now blank, each logged, and a gate refuses any new one. Six hundred and sixteen recipient names damaged by an encoding round-trip were restored. Eight foundations that file from outside the United States show their filed city and are named on the Explore panel instead of silently missing from every state total; eighteen with an unambiguous filed city gained their home state.

One vocabulary, again. A second word the terms page forbids, in all its forms, is gone from the static pages and from 240 per-book method notes; totals reconcile, cross-checks are checked. The recovered-rows page describes its own method in its own footer, and pages built from scans say a row carries a citation to the scanned filing rather than an IRS object ID.

Explore and the shortlist. City labels on a zoomed state no longer hide the largest city; Back and Forward keep the location filter in step with the address bar; the Foundation directory can save a funder; the research brief carries a median and largest grant for every foundation even without a saved selection; the dropdown is the primary control on a phone; state paths tab in alphabetical order. Dialogs return focus to the control that opened them in Safari as well; the page-number box works in Safari; Home and End move along the ledger tabs; a filed-total badge reads “Reconciles to Part XIV 3a” on every book that uses that referee.

Read back from the filings. Of the locations blanked as fragments, 103 on ten scanned books were re-read from the page images and restored; the 173 Johnson & Johnson recipient names that carried a stray quotation mark from an early OCR pass were read again from the register and corrected (553 rows); 115 e-filed filings were fetched from the IRS batch archives so their rows could be checked against the XML. What the filing itself does not give stays blank, and every change is logged with its source.

Small things a stranger would notice. A custom not-found page with a search box; a real social card on every page; a canonical address on every ledger; the print report shows a busy state on the largest books; the Typical grant range rounds consistently under $10,000. Six more deploy gates: fit-line shares, encoding artifacts, bulk-file columns, state totals, a wider doctrine-word sweep and a stricter location scan.

12 Sep 2026

Twelve strangers went through the site; forty-three things they hit are fixed

The search understands the name you type. “Annie E. Casey”, “Gordon and Betty Moore”, “Bill & Melinda Gates”, “Bloomberg Philanthropies”, “Arnold Ventures” and a misspelled “oishei foundatoin” all resolve to the right ledger. Matching is by word, middle initials and punctuation no longer break it, one typo per word is tolerated, four foundation hits show ranked by filed dollars, and the closest matches appear when nothing matches exactly. Escape keeps what you typed. A foundation can be saved to the shortlist straight from the search box.

A dead end is now a door. When a funder is not in the collection, the search says so and offers the same thing the outreach does: name it and one filed year is recovered, free, yours either way. The recovered-rows page opens with the same offer.

The pilot foundation’s page joins the fleet. The John R. Oishei Foundation ledger, the one page search engines index, was a stale August build that still used the word the terms page forbids. It is now built through the same pipeline as the other 1,951 pages, with the print report, the year rail, the Report a problem link and the current data.

One word, everywhere. The forbidden word is gone from the recovered-rows page (42 places), from the reconciliation figure’s spoken label on every ledger, from the no-script fallback, and from the privacy, exceptions and about pages. Where a page checks itself against its own code it says checked; where a total equals a filed line it says reconciled. A deploy gate now refuses a build that says otherwise.

The exceptions page is generated from the same sources as the ledgers. Every withheld or not-yet-published year and every single-row unitemized year is listed under its foundation, with an anchor, so the withheld chip on a ledger lands on its own entry. The Erie Family Foundation link resolves. The census CSV downloads.

Numbers that were wrong are right. Comcast NBCUniversal’s recipient count no longer counts a rounding note as a grantee; bracketed annotation rows on four other ledgers are unitemized rows, not recipients; fourteen Burroughs Wellcome rows and one MetLife row that carried a person’s name or an invalid code as the location were re-read from the filing or blanked; the Marriott and Caterpillar method notes agree with their own tie-outs; the tie-out’s spoken label names the referee the page actually uses; a stale FAIL flag on a reconciling year is recomputed at build. Hellman’s three Schwab spellings, its UCSF Foundation mailing variants and an OCR “Public Tand” are merged after review; the row keeps its filed text and the page says so.

Ledger pages read cleaner. “What it funds” never crowns a residual bucket; the print report carries the unitemized flag the screen carries; the Purpose column is labelled as filed; a filed year with no grants shows as such in the year rail; the Methodology crosswalk lost a placeholder header and both of its tables gained captions; every CSV export opens cleanly in Excel; the page-number box is a real touch target.

The hub on a phone. Funder cards no longer overlap or clip; View summary is reachable at every width; the explanation of why the results line differs from the headline total is visible; a saved-funder toast no longer covers a dialog.

Housekeeping. One footer wording on every static page, American spelling throughout, the collection date on the about page stamped from the database, and ten new deploy gates (doctrine words, placeholders, bracketed recipients, location codes, static links, downloads, exceptions anchors, roll-up claims, tie-out status, and a live-versus-staged diff run before any outreach).

6 Sep 2026

Individuals out of the index, no empty tie-out cards, a faster hub and lighter ledgers

Named individuals leave the index. Scholarship, fellowship, loan and hardship grants to named people stay on each foundation’s ledger page as filed, so every year still reconciles, and are withheld from search, cross-foundation profiles and the bulk download — 33,950 rows across 365 foundations. The rule and a removal path are on the privacy page.

No tie-out card without rows behind it. 71 years across 31 foundations showed a filed total as reconciled while the page carried no rows for that year. Each now carries a labeled not-itemized residual, and the deploy gate refuses the case.

The hub paints faster and reads plainer. The research list opens from a half-megabyte aggregate instead of a four-megabyte row index; the search index loads on the first keystroke. One name per view, “reconciled” is the only word used now, “matching dollars” everywhere the same figure appears, a keyboard-navigable search, and the “✓ Reconciled” mark on every result opens the reconciliation itself.

Ledger pages are light and lead with what a foundation funds. Books over 300 rows embed a preview and fetch the full list on demand (the largest page went from 4.1 MB to 110 KB); the overview opens with “What it funds”; focus rings are visible on every green control; phone toolbars no longer pin a third of the screen.

Location filter vetted. Recipient state codes are normalized once at build time; the filter reads United States, U.S. territories, Outside the U.S., Location not filed, with no guessed countries.

Every ledger answers “could we fit”. A Typical grant figure gives the middle half of named grants beside the median, and a one-line note states, from the record alone, what share of last year’s recipients were first-time and where the named dollars went. Withheld years now appear in the year rail with their reason, in order, instead of being silently absent; a filing newer than the last reconciled year reads “not yet published”.

Explore draws the state you pick. Selecting a state zooms the map to it and places its foundations as heat dots; a layout bug had left that panel blank. The research brief now carries three example grants for every shortlisted foundation, whether it was saved from the results list or the evidence panel.

Two more foundations. Elbridge Stuart Foundation and T. L. L. Temple Foundation, recovered from scanned attachments and reconciled to the dollar, join the fleet: 1,952 foundations, 1,568,035 rows, $308,266,607,168.

A report you can hand to a director. “Print report” on any ledger prints the headline figures, what the foundation funds, its largest recipients, the reconciliation to the filed totals, and every grant in the current scope — not the one page of sixty the tab happened to show. The year rail now appears as a strip on laptops and tablets where the sidebar does not fit, and a recipient opened from the hub lands on exactly that recipient’s merged record.

Long ledgers page by number. Books that run to hundreds of pages get First, Last and a typed page number beside Previous and Next. A link now carries the Recipients view as well (letter, sort, and whether the full list is open), the tax-year filter is one control instead of two, and the slash key opens the grant search from anywhere on the page. Every page now ends with a Report a problem link whose message carries the exact view you were looking at, and the hub’s search summary can copy its own link.
